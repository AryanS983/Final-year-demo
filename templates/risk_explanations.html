Risk Metrics Explained
Basic Risk Metrics
Alpha (α)
Measures fund's performance relative to expected return based on market risk (Beta). Positive alpha indicates outperformance.
Beta (β)
Measures sensitivity to market movements. β=1 means fund moves with market, β>1 is more volatile, β<1 is less volatile.
R-squared (R²)
Percentage of fund's movements explained by market movements. Higher R² indicates stronger correlation with benchmark.
Standard Deviation
Measures total risk/volatility. Higher values indicate more price fluctuations and higher risk.
Sharpe Ratio
Risk-adjusted return measure. Higher values indicate better return per unit of risk taken.
Advanced Risk Metrics
Sortino Ratio
Similar to Sharpe but only considers downside volatility. Better measure for risk-averse investors.
Maximum Drawdown
Largest peak-to-trough decline. Indicates worst-case scenario loss during the period.
Value at Risk (VaR)
Potential loss at 95% confidence level. "5% chance of losing more than X% in a day".
Conditional VaR (CVaR)
Average loss when VaR is exceeded. Measures tail risk beyond VaR threshold.
Information Ratio
Active return per unit of tracking error. Measures skill of fund manager.
Calmar Ratio
Annual return divided by maximum drawdown. Measures return relative to worst losses.

Positive/Negative Days Analysis

Positive Days %: Percentage of trading days with positive returns. Higher values indicate more consistent performance.
Negative Days %: Percentage of trading days with negative returns. Lower values are preferable.
Interpretation: A fund with 55%+ positive days is considered good. Consistency matters more than individual day performance.

Benchmark Comparison

Compares fund performance against market benchmark (Nifty 50). Key indicators:

  • Return Difference: Fund return minus benchmark return
  • Volatility Difference: Fund risk minus benchmark risk
  • Sharpe Difference: Fund's risk-adjusted performance vs benchmark
  • Outperformed Benchmark: Whether fund beat the market

💡 Risk Assessment Tips
  • Conservative Investors: Look for Low Risk, Sharpe Ratio > 1.0, Max Drawdown < 15%
  • Moderate Investors: Medium Risk acceptable, Sharpe Ratio 0.5-1.0, focus on consistent returns
  • Aggressive Investors: Can tolerate High Risk if compensated by high returns, check Alpha & Information Ratio
  • All Investors: Avoid funds with negative Alpha, very high Beta (>1.5), or frequent large drawdowns
